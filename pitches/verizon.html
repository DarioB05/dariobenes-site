Thesis

The pricing war is largely in the rearview mirror, and the Street is still anchored to the past.

A combination of regulatory issues and intense competition pushed Verizon to near $31 in late 2023. The stock recovered slowly and averaged about $40 through 2025.

Thesis I, the pricing war is ending: the team visited six Verizon, AT&T and T-Mobile stores across Lee, Collier and Palm Beach counties, built a Claude Code scraper over five years of Wayback Machine pricing data, and ran sentiment analysis on two years of earnings calls. All three showed carriers moving to premium lines and phone-plus-internet bundles, the start of a rational triopoly the Street had not modeled.

Thesis II, a reborn Verizon: the roughly $20 billion all-cash Frontier acquisition closed in January 2026 with $500mm of expected annual run-rate cost savings. Convergence bundles of phone plus home internet raise margins, improve lock-in and lower churn. New CEO Dan Schulman is running a $5 billion cost savings plan.

Street view vs our view

Where the team disagreed with consensus

The Street’s view

  • Near-term headwinds dominate the story
  • Telecom competition compresses pricing indefinitely
  • Growth is structurally capped

Our view

  • The pricing war is ending, unmodeled by the Street
  • Frontier is a game-changer on cost and lock-in
  • Sell-side revisions are being raised aggressively

Catalysts

Catalyst path

  • 2Q26, capital return inflection: the $25 billion buyback program in full effect
  • FY26, free cash flow expansion: capex drifts down as the C-Band buildout ends, against $21 billion of FCF guidance
  • FY27, deleveraging and synergies: unsecured debt to adjusted EBITDA reverts to the long-term target as Frontier synergies are realized

Risks

Key risks and mitigants

Debt load

Risk: A highly leveraged balance sheet from C-Band spectrum and the 5G build-out; higher rates would raise debt service costs and pressure free cash flow.

Mitigant: Peak capex is largely behind Verizon, and management is cutting costs through synergies and a restructuring.

Pricing power

Risk: ARPU and ARPA fell over recent quarters, postpaid net adds missed, and churn trended up.

Mitigant: Store visits and alternative data showed aggressive share-stealing had cooled; convergence and myPlan should lower churn.

Execution

Risk: Fiber-to-the-home passing targets could be constrained by supply chains or labor, and incumbents could restart aggressive promotions.

Mitigant: Management has consistently met or exceeded fiber build targets, and fiber is structurally superior to legacy cable.
