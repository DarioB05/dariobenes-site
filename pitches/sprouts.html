Thesis

A disappointing Q3 made the market forget how consistently Sprouts compounds.

The market valued Sprouts as a basic, generic grocer. Despite double-digit sales growth, investors reacted poorly to the Q3 2025 guidance reset and treated investments in customer loyalty as costs without a payoff.

Margins should improve as Sprouts expands around its distribution centers, opens smaller and more efficient stores, and moves to self-distribution. About 80% of stores are within 250 miles of a distribution center, and new stores target 21,000 to 25,000 square feet versus a 28,000 square foot average.

Combine that with a conservative balance sheet, a $1 billion buyback authorization, and a loyalty program launched in Q3 2025, and EPS growth accelerates. Q3 2025 gross margin was 38.7%, up 60 basis points year over year, while the company absorbed the cost of those investments.

Street view vs our view

Where the team disagreed with consensus

The Street’s view

  • Q3 showed the growth story beginning to break
  • Unclear payoff from loyalty and cost-incurring investments
  • Grocery is too competitive for durable growth

Our view

  • Q3 was a recalibration of expectations, not a drop in performance
  • Loyalty and mix shift lift margins and deepen stickiness
  • Sprouts’ farmers-market premise is genuinely differentiated

Valuation

Scenarios and price target

Base DCF at a 6.9% WACC, consistent with Sprouts’ low-beta, low-leverage balance sheet, with mid-teens near-term free cash flow growth and a terminal value grounded in peer multiples of 6.5x to 10.5x EV/EBITDA. The bull case assumes faster store expansion, loyalty adoption and supply-chain savings; the bear case values Sprouts closer to a traditional grocer.

Catalysts

Catalyst path

  • Store growth: 35 to 37 new stores a year, 140 more locations already approved, toward a 1,000-store national goal
  • Self-distribution of fresh meat and seafood, projected to be complete in the second quarter of 2026
  • Loyalty program (launched Q3 2025) expanding first-party customer data from 11% toward the roughly 80% coverage of competitors
  • Private label: 350+ new Sprouts brand products rolling out in 2025

Risks

Key risks and mitigants

Consumer spending softens

Risk: A broader industry slowdown.

Mitigant: Lean on resilient higher-income customers to protect traffic.

Tighter margins

Risk: Category inflation compresses grocery margins.

Mitigant: Expanded self-distribution and rigorous category management.

Differentiation at risk

Risk: Competitors expand natural and organic ranges.

Mitigant: Protect the unique assortment and reinforce the health-focused brand.
